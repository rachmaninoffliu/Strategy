# Leveraged-ETF pairs: TQQQ/QQQ, SOXL/SMH, SPXL/SPY - statistical arbitrage on intraday bars

Status 2026-10-25: research, backtest app and paper/live engine built, independently reviewed by Valid and RM, tested (147 tests in three files); the fill accounting and the trade table were re-checked on 2026-10-25 (section 2b). **No order has ever been sent to any account. Valid has rejected it for capital; the backtest does not make money.**

## 1. Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is there a tradable stat-arb edge in these three pairs? | **No.** With fills at the open of the bar after the signal and quoted-spread costs, **all 1,458 rule sets lose money** (best in-sample Sharpe -0.85; positive in-sample: 0) on 1 / 5 / 15 / 30-minute bars. Valid re-ran six rows from the raw bars and an independent re-implementation of the 5-minute TQQQ/QQQ trades: identical entries, exits and costs, no look-ahead, no accounting error that flips the sign. |
| Is there any reversion at all? | Yes, tiny: a Sharpe of about 4.3 on 1-minute bars and 1.6 on 5-minute bars **if trading were free**, i.e. **0.5-1 bp of gross profit per trade**. A pair round trip costs **4.7 bp (TQQQ/QQQ), 5.7 (SPXL/SPY), 11.1 (SOXL/SMH)** because the hedge leg is 3x larger. Break-even needs about **10% of the assumed costs (about 0.5 bp a round trip)** - spread-earning (passive) fills, not market orders. |
| Which time frame is best? | None clears the costs. By net Sharpe the least bad is 30 minutes (-2.0, it trades least); by gross edge it is 1 minute (+4.3 at zero cost, -8.5 after costs). The default research setting is 5 minutes. |
| What looked like an edge? | A bar-based backtest that fills at the signal bar's last trade shows Sharpe +2.9 (5 min) to +5.9 (1 min): 8-12 bp per trade of gross profit that vanishes when the same trades fill at the next open (section 2b explains where it comes from). Valid: it exceeds the quoted bounce (1-4 bp), so it is stale / asynchronous last prints, lead-lag between the legs and selection of extremes, not bounce alone; "not capturable at retail latency" stands. |
| **The open question** | **The fill price.** With fill = close_t + a x (open_t+1 - close_t): a = 1 (next open, the project's assumption) Sharpe -4.9; a = 0.5 -0.7 (now a built-in fill model, 'mid'); a = 0.25 +1.4 (Valid's sweep); a = 0 +2.9. Quote-based signals with NBBO fills at the signal timestamps would settle it. |
| Can it run live on this account? | Only as a DRY RUN. ## 2. What was built

`../LevETF-Pairs` (see `README.md`): Alpaca 1-minute SIP and IEX bars for the six ETFs 2016-2026 (about 1.05M bars each), a vectorised signal stage, a bar-by-bar rules/accounting stage, a one-call bridge (backtest, time-frame scan,
ablation, cost and threshold checks), a session engine (DRY RUN shadow book or ARMED two-leg market orders), and a PyQt6 app with the same layout as the Stat-Arb app: settings panel + Live trading / Backtest / Signals & risk / How it works.
Extras specific to this strategy: **Time frames** scan, **Spread viewer** (one session, spread and s-score with the holdings shaded), a **fill-model switch** (next_open / mid / close) with a **Fill models** tab that shows the three side by side, a **Trades** tab that says for every round trip which product was bought and which sold, when, and at what prices, per-pair weights, cost-of-a-round-trip table.

## 2b. Re-check of the fill accounting and the trade table (2026-10-25)

The user saw a huge gap between 'execute at open' and 'execute at close' and found the Trades tab ambiguous. What was checked and changed:
- **The decisions do not depend on the fill price**, so the three fill models trade exactly the same round trips (Fill models tab: identical trade counts); only the price the orders get differs. The gap is the move of the *spread* between the signal bar's last print and the next bar's open: entering at the old close collects the snap-back of two stale prints, exiting at the old close avoids the move against the position. On the default preset that is worth about 10 bp a trip (gross +10.1 bp at the close vs +0.7 bp at the next open), which is the whole difference between Sharpe +2.9 and -4.9. No order sent after the bar closes can get the old close.
- **Exact accounting.** The P&L used constant-notional bar returns (the position rebalanced to the same dollar amount each bar). It now follows fixed shares from the fill price, as a real account does: per $1 of the 3x leg at entry, long/short `(L_exit/L_entry - 1) - beta (B_exit/B_entry - 1)`. The Trades tab lists the four fill prices, and `price_bp` recomputed from them equals `gross_bp` for every one of the 2,488 default-preset trades (all three pairs) under all three fills, to 1e-11 bp (a test asserts it on 5-minute TQQQ/QQQ data). Versus the earlier accounting the Sharpe moved by at most 0.3 (e.g. 15-minute next open -3.09 -> -3.37, 5-minute -4.88 -> -4.89) and no conclusion changed; the earlier tables are kept in `reports/old_scan_constant_notional/`. This also resolves Valid's note that the constant-notional return was not what a real account earns.
- **Fill models.** `next_open` (a = 1, the default), `mid` (a = 0.5, half way between the signal close and the next open, Sharpe -0.73 on the default preset, out of sample -0.69) and `close` (a = 0, optimistic, +2.85). The fill price is F = close_t + a (open_t+1 - close_t); the old position earns close -> F and the new one F -> close.
- **Trades tab.** One row per round trip: date, pair, **'LONG TQQQ / SHORT QQQ'** (or the reverse), the orders at entry per $10,000 of the 3x ETF ('BUY TQQQ $10,000 + SELL QQQ $30,000'), entry and exit clock time, minutes held, the entry and exit price of both legs, gross and net bp, net dollars per $10,000, the s-score at entry and why it closed (target, stop, time stop, end of day). The Live tab uses the same wording: the open positions read 'LONG TQQQ 357 sh / SHORT QQQ 107 sh', and the decision log says 'ENTER long spread: BUY 357 TQQQ @ 70.12 + SELL 107 QQQ @ 612.40' / 'EXIT: ...'.
- **Intraday-A3C** was put through the same audit (`Intraday-A3C/DESIGN.md` section 10): the basket Sharpe is 1.54 at a close fill and 1.55 at a next-open fill, because a single liquid stock has no stale-print spread; its decision log now says 'BUY 3 lots = 105 sh TSLA @ price' and 'LONG 2 lots = 70 sh TSLA'.

## 3. Method

- **Spread.** A daily-reset 3x ETF is 3x its index since the previous close. `X_t = (L_t/L_close - 1) - beta (B_t/B_close - 1)` (beta 3, or a rolling regression shrunk toward 3). Alternative `chain`: the paper's cumulative bar residual, overnight excluded.
  Caveat (Valid): 96-97% of the variance of the 'reset' spread is *between* sessions (a persistent offset, probably the noisy prior-close print used as the NAV reference); the within-session s.d. is only 2.8-2.9 bp, below the round-trip cost. An offset-free variant (demeaned by the first bars) still earns 0.7-1.4 bp gross and loses 3-5 bp net, so the verdict is unchanged, but the "structural" rationale of the default is weaker than the preset note suggested.
- **Score.** Rolling AR(1) fit of X over `win_days` sessions (transitions across the overnight boundary excluded): half-life, equilibrium level and sd, `s = (X - m)/sigma_eq`. Vectorised with rolling sums, checked against a brute-force window loop and independently re-implemented by Valid (max difference 1.6e-8).
- **Rules.** Long the spread (buy $1 of the 3x ETF, short $beta of the 1x ETF) at s < -open, short at s > +open, exit at the close levels, model-failure stop with a cooldown, time stop, half-life filter, expected-capture / round-trip-cost filter, no entry in the first 15 / last 30 minutes, flat 10 minutes before the close.
- **Fills and costs.** `next_open`: signal from the closes of bar t, fill at the open of bar t+1, the old position earns close t -> open t+1 and the new one open t+1 -> close; fixed shares from the fill price (section 2b). Cost per unit position change = half the SIP-sampled quoted spread + 0.3 bp slippage on the 3x leg plus beta times the same on the 1x leg.
  Spreads (bp, full, median of six windows through the session on three recent sessions, applied flat to 2017-2026 - earlier lower prices mean wider bp spreads, so history is if anything lenient): QQQ 0.28, SPY 0.26, TQQQ 1.41, SMH 1.93, SPXL 2.51, SOXL 2.95.
  The entry filter always prices a trade at the base costs, so sweeping the slippage changes what trades cost, not which trades are taken (Valid found the earlier sweep confounded).
- **Sizing.** Unit-gross return of the book, equal or inverse-vol pair weights, EWMA volatility target on the strategy's own past returns (10%), gross capped at 3x. Note (Valid): the target never binds - realised vol is 0.7% against 10%, so G sits at the 3x cap on 99% of days and the reported CAGR / max drawdown are artefacts of the cap; the Sharpe is unaffected. The live engine caps G at 1.
- **Selection.** Rule sets are ranked on 2017-2021 only; 2022-2026 is shown beside and never used to rank.

**F. IEX versus SIP signals** (`reports/feed_test.log`, 2020-08 to 2026-10, default preset): s-score correlation 0.29 / 0.46 / 0.24 (TQQQ/QQQ, SOXL/SMH, SPXL/SPY) on 5-minute bars, 0.27 / 0.40 / 0.20 on 15, 0.27 / 0.57 / 0.20 on 30. IEX has a bar in only 75% / 92% (TQQQ, QQQ), 64% / 59% (SOXL, SMH), 31% / 98% (SPXL, SPY) of the minutes the consolidated tape has one.
Trading on IEX signals but marking on SIP prices: Sharpe -13.7 (5 min), -10.2 (15), -6.7 (30), against -4.3, -3.4, -1.8 on SIP signals over the same years, with 8-12x as many round trips. (The single-stock strategy of `Intraday-A3C` survived IEX with edge correlation 0.98-0.99; a *spread of two stale prints* does not.)

**G. Why SIP and IEX data change the result so much** (`reports/feed_gap.log`, `research/feed_gap.py`; one session 2026-10-23 10:00-14:00 ET, 48 five-minute boundaries, and the 5-minute backtest 2020-09 .. 2026-10 in each world)
- IEX is one exchange: 1-3% of the trades of these ETFs (QQQ 24 trades a minute against 1,774 on the consolidated tape, TQQQ 14 / 776, SOXL 17 / 1,835, SPXL 0.9 / 53). At a 5-minute boundary the last IEX trade is a median 4.1 s old for QQQ, 5.8 s for TQQQ, 3.5 s for SOXL and **118 s for SPXL** (SIP: 0.06 / 0.12 / 0.09 / 2.2 s); the first trade of the next bar comes a median 3.0 / 4.4 / 6.3 / **157 s** later (SIP 0.03 / 0.09 / 0.04 / 1.7 s). Empty 5-minute bars: SPXL 22% on IEX, TQQQ 4%, SOXL 5%, SMH 6.5%, none on SIP.
- So an IEX "close" and "next open" are seconds to minutes apart and each is a print of 1-3% of the market. The spread built from two such series jumps by 9.5 bp (TQQQ/QQQ), 20.8 (SOXL/SMH) and 12.8 (SPXL/SPY) on average from close to next open, against 2.0 / 6.6 / 2.6 bp on SIP.
- Backtest on SIP bars (1,046 trades): next open Sharpe -5.29, midpoint -1.32, close +2.26; entry +5.96 bp in the trade's favour between the signal close and the next open, exit -1.84. Backtest on IEX bars (6,878 trades, 6.6x as many): next open **+1.30**, midpoint +11.34, close +13.75; the move in the trade's favour is +27.8 bp at entry. An IEX-only backtest looks profitable because the next IEX print is itself a stale print that reverts, a profit nobody can trade: orders execute against the consolidated market (marked at real prices the same IEX signals earn Sharpe -13.7, section F).
- Consequence for the live engine: signals from IEX bars are mostly stale-print noise, and fills happen at consolidated prices. A paper test on IEX signals measures plumbing, fills and the feed gap; for the strategy itself the signals need real-time SIP (`LIVE_TEST_RUNBOOK.md` section 0).

## 5. Time-frame decision

The user asked for the best intraday time frame. By net result: **30 minutes** (Sharpe -2.0, out-of-sample -1.9) because it trades least; by gross edge: **1 minute**. Neither is positive. If the strategy is pursued, 5 minutes is the setting that balances edge (Sharpe 1.6 gross) against noise and it
is the default; it needs (a) real-time SIP data, (b) execution that earns rather than pays the spread (passive limit orders), and (c) a measured fill cost and fill price - none of which a bar-based backtest can supply. The paper / DRY-RUN engine records exactly that (fill log with slippage against the signal price).
Valid's recommended next experiment: quote-mid signals with NBBO-based fills at the signal timestamps - it settles the fill price and whether passive execution can earn the spread.

## 6. Engine, safety, and what the independent reviews found

112 tests (`scripts/test_levpairs.py`; plus 12 in `test_levpairs_compare.py` and 23 in `test_levpairs_ops.py`, section 6b): rolling OU = brute-force windows; no look-ahead in reset / chain / z modes (perturbing later bars leaves earlier scores unchanged; the hedge ratio only sees earlier sessions); the state machine on hand-made cases; a hand-computed long AND short trade under all three fill models with prices on both legs (fixed shares, the hedge ratio, costs on four fills); the trade table names the long and the short product, the clock times and the fill prices, and its P&L is reproducible from them on real data for every fill; the three fills trade identical round trips and are ordered next_open < mid < close; the live position and action text; costs on both legs; sweeping costs does not change which trades are taken; nothing held overnight;
**live signals == backtest signals**; **a replayed session reproduces the backtest's positions and P&L**; DRY RUN never touches the broker; the two-leg order flow with a fake broker; the GUI offscreen including a whole backtest through the Backtest tab.

**RM's review found (fakes only) and the code now handles:** a polling error after both legs were sent left the ledger flat while the broker held shares - now the intent is written to the state file before the first order, polling is guarded (3 errors = status unknown, pair BROKEN, alarm), the broker is reconciled against the ledger every bar and a pair with an unexplained position is blocked, a restart with an order in flight or unexplained positions refuses to start;
flatten used to clear the ledger even when closes failed - now it re-reads the broker and a pair leaves the ledger only when the closing fills are visible, otherwise it stays BROKEN with an alarm; the hard flatten (15:52) is checked before the end-of-session break, a `finally` flattens on Stop / error, the window close waits for it; the GUI locked its ARM / account controls only by un-ticking them (KILL then built an empty unarmed engine) - now the controls are locked while a session runs, the status bar follows the running engine, KILL reaches the running engine under the engine lock;
exposure: the volatility-target gross saturated and the caps were mis-computed (other pairs' shares valued at this pair's price) - now caps are share-of-equity based (gross 25%, single leg 10%, entries are scaled down to fit), each leg at its own price, gross multiplier capped at 1; pre-flight is a gate for ARMED sessions; single-instance lock, unique client ids, an open order blocks a second one; the IEX guard is inside the order path and the GUI needs a typed phrase.
**RM's re-verification** passed the GUI locking / KILL routing, the exposure caps (0 violations over 84 configurations, worst gross 25.0% of NAV, worst leg 9.9%), the pre-flight gate and the IEX guard, and found five remaining gaps, of which RM's second pass PASSED the unknown-leg handling, KILL, the lock, the hedge guard and the GUI items, and found two more (a HIGH defect in my new orphan flatten and a count-based retry). Fixed afterwards and covered by tests (**RM's third pass passed them**): the orphan flatten moved the broker toward the ledger (it would have bought shares missing from the broker, or re-opened a pair closed by hand) - it now trades only the broker's excess, never toward the ledger; the end-of-day flatten retried a fixed 6 times (about 25 s) - it now retries until 15:58:30; KILL pressed between the two legs of an entry now unwinds the first leg. Earlier second-round items: an unknown leg state still triggered an unwind of the other leg (a naked hedge) - now nothing is unwound when any leg is unknown; only two back-to-back flatten attempts - now up to 6 with a 5 s back-off until 15:58:30; a flatten could stack a second order on a symbol whose cancel failed - now it re-lists the open orders and skips those symbols; KILL could wait behind a hung step - now the lock wait is bounded and a kill flag refuses entries in flight; no route closed unexplained positions - added a typed-phrase **Flatten unexplained positions** action (and an opt-in 15:52 version for a dedicated account);
plus the non-atomic lock file, the whole-share hedge-ratio guard (skip when the ratio is off by more than 10%) and a KILL warning when the armed ledger on disk differs from the book being killed.

**RM's third pass (fakes only)** passed the orphan flatten (an exhaustive truth table of 2,401 ledger x broker combinations, both signs: 0 violations), the persistent end-of-day flatten, KILL between the legs, restart resume, the replay against the backtest (2.5e-8 per trade) and "DRY RUN never touches the broker", and found seven defects. All were fixed on 2026-10-25 and each has a test that was checked to FAIL with its fix removed (79 tests):
- **D1 (HIGH for real money):** KILL, after its 5 s wait for the step lock, ran a second flatten beside a still-looping one; with a broker outage and API-like latency 11 of 24 trials double-closed and left TQQQ -100 / QQQ +30. Now only one flatten runs at a time (a second caller waits for the running one, then makes one more pass; the orphan flatten takes the same guard), and the position and the open orders of each symbol are re-read right before its close order.
- **D2:** a state file from the previous build (no entry prices) loaded mid-session faked a -$2,000 first-bar loss and a false loss halt, and a NaN in one price field made the day P&L NaN so the halt failed open. Entry prices are seeded from `entry_px`, a bar with a missing price books nothing, a P&L that is not a number now HALTS, and the shadow book catches up bars skipped by a restart.
- **D3:** the EXIT text came from the ledger, not from what was sent ("SELL 100" when 60 were sent, or none), and entry / exit showed the signal close. The text is now built from the legs actually sent with the fill prices (a dry run says its prices are the signal close; a partial close adds "the ledger held ..."), a halt row's "Position after" is FLAT, and the decisions / fills CSVs have one fixed column layout (the first row's header used to make pandas fail on the later rows).
- **D4:** after Replay, KILL / "Flatten unexplained positions" / Reconcile in the GUI acted through the unarmed replay engine and sent nothing. They now use the running session's engine, else an engine built from the account and mode shown in the controls, never a replay engine.
- **D5:** close orders could be sent while the market was closed (queueing for the next open, able to flip a position closed meanwhile). Every close now needs the broker clock to say open and the 15:58:30 cutoff not to have passed; otherwise the engine's queued orders are cancelled, the pair stays in the ledger as BROKEN with an alarm, and an orphan close that did not fill is cancelled.
- **D6:** the engine lock's heartbeat is refreshed inside the flatten loops (a 6.5 minute flatten no longer outlives the 180 s lock).
- **D7:** the hedge-leg rounding could overshoot the 10% leg cap by a share (worst 10.22% of NAV over 139,813 random sizings; so the earlier "0 violations over 84 configurations" was too strong); sizing now steps down until both caps hold with whole shares (a 3,000-sizing test asserts none). Also: the Decisions table tolerates frames without a note column.

## 6b. The paper-test machinery (2026-10-26; not yet reviewed by RM)

Built for a month of ARMED trading on the Alpaca paper account (`LIVE_TEST_RUNBOOK.md`):
- **Both legs at the same moment.** `send()` used to submit the first leg, wait for the answer, then the second. Both legs now go out from two threads released together by a barrier (a leg that cannot be built stops the other: nothing is sent; a submit that has not returned after 20 s is an unknown order, nothing is unwound). Measured with a fake broker that takes 0.3 s per call: the two orders arrive within milliseconds of each other and the entry takes one round trip (0.35 s), not two. Consequences handled: a rejected leg unwinds the filled one (the unwinds also go out together), a KILL pressed while the orders are in flight unwinds the entry (before the send: nothing is sent), the pre-trade reads (broker positions, open orders) run in parallel. The earlier "KILL between the legs" case no longer exists.
- **A journal of every order.** `levpairs_fills_<mode>.jsonl` (the record; also a `.csv`, which an open Excel window can lock without losing a row) has one row per order sent - entries, exits, unwinds, KILL / end-of-day / orphan closes, also rejected and unknown ones - with the decision it belongs to (`trip`, `j`, `s`, `decided_at`), order id, client id, local submit / ack / seen times in ns, the broker's `submitted_at` / `filled_at`, status, fill price, quantity, signal price and slippage. Decisions are journaled the same way with their wall-clock time, and each session writes `levpairs_run_<mode>_<date>.json` with its exact configuration. After the close `sync_broker_day()` stores the broker's own order list and fills.
- **Live vs backtest** (`levpairs_compare.py`, read-only): per session it builds the live round trips (fixed-share P&L from the real fills, which product was long and which short) and two backtest twins of the same session with the same configuration, on the final SIP bars and on the final IEX bars; matches the trades; compares fills against the signal close, the twin's next-open price, the SIP NBBO mid and touch at the moment the broker accepted the order, and the first SIP trade after it; compares the s-score and position of every bar with both twins; and measures the latency stack. Output: `reports/live_compare/` (REPORT_<date>.md, trip / leg / decision tables, summary.csv, MONTH_TO_DATE.md).
- **Daily job and watchdog.** `levpairs_daily.py day` (PAPER only; DRY RUN unless `--arm "ARM PAPER"`, plus `--accept-iex "ACCEPT IEX"` on IEX signals) waits for 09:25 ET, runs the session, then runs the evening routine; `kill`, `unkill`, `flatten`, `status`, `post` for the rest. `levpairs_watchdog.py` is a separate process that never acts while the engine's heartbeat is fresh, alerts when positions are open without a heartbeat or after 15:56:30, and closes the ledger positions of a dead engine only with `--flatten "FLATTEN PAPER"` (unexplained positions only with a second phrase).
- **Tests (fakes only, no orders).** 6 new order-flow tests in `test_levpairs.py`; `test_levpairs_compare.py` fabricates a whole live session from the SIP twin with known slippage (0.5 bp every fill, a KILL exit, a failed entry, the broker's record overriding the journal, a torn last line) and checks that the comparison reports exactly those numbers; `test_levpairs_ops.py` covers the arming phrases, waiting for the open in New York time, the evening routine surviving a failing step, the watchdog's decision table and closing, and a **rehearsal**: the ARMED engine runs a real past session bar by bar against a fake broker that fills at the next open, and the comparison finds it identical to the SIP backtest (same trips, positions, no slippage).
- Never run against the real broker (the `/account/activities/FILL` read goes through the client's raw GET: alpaca-py 0.43 has no method for it). RM reviewed all of the above on 2026-10-26 (fakes only); its findings and the fixes follow.

**RM's review of the paper-test machinery** (fakes only; 93 + 5 + 11 tests passing at the time). **Passed:** the broker sync (request, NY-day `after` / `until` for EDT and EST, ALL / ASC / limit 500, LP- filter, GET only, throttled, places and cancels nothing); the journal (a failing `append_log`, a locked csv, a ValueError left the entry, the ledger and the jsonl intact; written after the write-ahead state and before the ledger update; 2 ms of extra I/O before the first order); the monotonic throttle (N4); the two threads' submits (0.0 ms apart, write-ahead on disk first, a rejected leg unwinds the other, an unbuildable leg sends nothing, both hung = both unknown and nothing unwound, KILL in flight unwinds both, partial fills unwound, unique client ids); the caps (0 overshoots in 139,812 sizings, ten fault-injected sessions at most 24.8% gross and 9.97% per leg); no route to a real-money account in the daily job; exact arming phrases; a second `day` stopped by the lock; the watchdog's decision table. **Defects found and fixed** (each with tests that were checked to fail without the fix where an engine behaviour is involved):
- **D1 (MED) an ambiguous submit result was recorded as rejected / unknown while the order was live.** alpaca-py retries a POST on 504 / 429; the retry answers "client_order_id must be unique"; the leg was journaled as rejected, the other leg unwound and a naked QQQ -107 was left (reproduced). A submit that hung past the 20 s join and landed later left a position with a flat ledger, and the intent was cleared. Now after any error that may hide a success (no HTTP answer, 5xx, 408, 429, "must be unique") the engine asks the broker for the order by its client id (`get_order_by_client_id`): found = the entry goes on with it; absent (404) = a real rejection; lookup failed = unknown. A plain 4xx is not looked up. An unknown order keeps the intent on disk (`pending.unknown`), the client ids of unknown legs are journaled, only a verified orphan flatten clears it, and the armed daily job now runs the 15:52 orphan flatten by default.
- **D2 (MED) Ctrl+C or an exception during an entry in flight left the legs open with a flat ledger** (reproduced). `run_session`'s clean-up (`_leave_session`) now also closes whatever an intent still on disk left at the broker.
- **D3 (MED) alpaca-py makes its HTTP calls without a timeout**, so a stalled call would freeze the engine. `D.harden()` gives the trading and data clients' sessions a default (5 s connect, 20 s read) timeout.
- **Journal / state (LOW):** the decisions-log write and the journal-row building can no longer raise into a step or an unwind; `save_state` retries the replace when Windows refuses it for a moment (the watchdog has the file open); the 500-orders warning tests the unfiltered list and the 100-activities page limit is flagged.
- **C1 (MED)** a trip closed by the watchdog was not recognised as closed (its P&L was dropped): every journal action that is not ENTER / EXIT / UNWIND is now a flatten's close order. **C2 (MED)** an exit split over two orders was priced at the last order only ($330 reported against a true $210): fills of a trip are aggregated quantity-weighted and the P&L uses the quantities exited. **C3 (MED)** with IEX noise "slippage vs twin" mostly measured timing: slippage and `d_price_bp` are now judged only on comparable trips (same entry bar AND exit bar, one go, same quantity); the others are counted and priced separately (`d_price_bp_all`). **C4 (LOW)** a reused trip id made `live_trips` raise: trip ids are per entry attempt (`-1`, `-2` ...) and duplicates are merged instead of raising. **C5 (LOW)** the summary and the month-to-date were keyed by date only, so DRY RUN days polluted the paper month: they are keyed by (date, mode) with per-mode files.
- **Watchdog (MED): no exclusion between closers in two processes** (two closers within 250 ms double-closed in 29 of 60 trials, 60 of 60 with 0.5 s of position lag). A shared lock file (`levpairs_flatten_<mode>.lock`, created with O_EXCL, touched at every step of a flatten, a dead closer's lock expires after 90 s) now serialises the engine's and the watchdog's flattens. The watchdog acts only after "dead" is seen on two looks 20 s apart and forgets what it saw after a gap (a PC that slept), leaves a marker file that a revived engine honours (it reloads the flat ledger and stops for the day), closes the legs of an entry that was in flight with `--flatten` alone, alerts with a pop-up and the ALERT file, and alerts when no engine is running on a trading day (the broker's clock is asked, so holidays do not alarm). Heartbeats are also touched inside `send`.
- **Daily job / runbook:** `flatten --arm "ARM PAPER"` no longer demands the IEX phrase; `post` can read the paper files of an earlier day (`--mode paper`) without arming; yesterday's leftovers (positions or an in-flight order) are closed at 09:30:30 before the session starts (it stops with an error if it cannot); Ctrl+Break and a plain `taskkill` / SIGTERM flatten like Ctrl+C; the runbook now says: own API keys through `ALPACA_API_KEY` / `ALPACA_SECRET_KEY` (the default keys are the account the Claude tools use), Task Scheduler's power conditions off and a time limit of 12 hours or more, never close the console, paper equity of at least $25,000.

## 7. Team positions

- **QR:** the effect exists (zero-cost Sharpe 4.3 / 1.6 / 0.9 / 0.2 at 1 / 5 / 15 / 30 minutes) but it is microstructure: sub-1 bp per trade. Same lesson as Stats-arb: raw signal alive in gross terms, dead in net. The fill price, not the signal, is the open question.
- **DA:** the first scan looked excellent (Sharpe 5-9) because last-trade closes were treated as fills; that is the finding to remember. IEX is not a substitute for the tape for two-leg spreads. Spreads were sampled on three sessions; data is split-adjusted only (ex-dividend days distort 37-48 sessions of the spread offset, immaterial to P&L).
- **Dev:** engine and app built and tested; no GUI test can read text (the offscreen renderer has no fonts), layout was checked on screenshots.
- **Valid (independent):** no P&L or look-ahead error found; verdict confirmed; paper trading acceptable only as a fill-cost measurement harness; real capital rejected.
- **RM (independent, five passes):** DRY RUN approved; ARMED paper acceptable supervised on a dedicated account with one pair, broker checked at 15:55; 'Flatten unexplained positions' in market hours on a dedicated account (it refuses safely while a step is running); the 15:52 orphan option may be on for a dedicated paper account; D1-D7 and N1-N3 verified fixed (KILL during a flatten is safe); unattended use, auto-start (no external 15:55 watchdog) and a real account barred.
- **QT:** to make this work the cost has to fall from about 5 bp to about 0.5 bp per round trip: limit orders at the touch with queue risk, SIP data, probably only TQQQ/QQQ.
- **PM:** no capital; do not run it armed; keep the app as a measurement tool.
